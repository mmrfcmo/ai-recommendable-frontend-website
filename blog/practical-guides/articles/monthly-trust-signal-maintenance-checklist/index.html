The Monthly Trust Signal Maintenance Checklist

Published 7 October 2026 · Trust Signal Framework™ editorial

Digital representation decays quietly. Profiles change, staff move, services evolve, pages go stale and old descriptions stay live. A monthly routine catches small changes before they become a bigger problem.

Monthly identity checks

Evidence and profile checks The useful test is whether someone can understand the point without needing insider knowledge about how your business works.

A repeatable routine for keeping a digital representation accurate and useful. That makes this subject practical rather than theoretical: the objective is to reduce uncertainty by improving the information and evidence available around the organisation.

A practical way to approach it

Start with what a sensible prospect, partner or third party would need to know. Then compare that requirement with what your current digital record actually provides.

  • Monthly basics
  • Quarterly deep review
  • Change management

What good looks like

Quarterly deeper reviews A strong approach is usually specific rather than louder. It helps the reader reach a clearer conclusion using information that can be checked and understood.

For example, instead of saying that a business is “trusted”, show the evidence or context that allows someone to assess that statement. Instead of relying on a broad service label, explain what is actually delivered, for whom and in what circumstances. Instead of adding another generic page, strengthen the page that already answers an important customer question.

Common mistakes to avoid

  1. Confusing more content with better information. More pages do not automatically create more understanding.
  2. Using broad claims without context. Precision makes information easier to assess.
  3. Letting important details drift. Old names, locations, service labels or descriptions can remain live long after the business has changed.
  4. Treating one source as the whole picture. A strong website is important, but digital representation is distributed across multiple sources.

A simple test you can run today

Open your website as if you had never seen the business before. Give yourself five minutes. Write down what you believe the business is, what it does, who it serves, where it operates and what evidence supports its claims. Then compare your notes with the pages and sources the business publishes.

Any answer that required guesswork is worth investigating. Any answer that changed depending on the page you visited is worth investigating even more.

Where this fits within the Trust Signal Framework™

Practical Guides is one part of the six-signal framework used by Recommendable by AI. The wider assessment also considers Entity Clarity, Knowledge Completeness, Trust Evidence, Technical Accessibility, Narrative Consistency and External Validation as interconnected parts of digital representation.

The point is not to promise an outcome from a particular search engine or AI system. It is to make the underlying representation of the business clearer, more complete, more consistent and better supported.
